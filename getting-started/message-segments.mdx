A **message segment** is how mobile carriers measure the length of an SMS text message. Understanding message segments can help you manage messaging costs, avoid delivery issues, and communicate more effectively with your customers.

## What Is a Message Segment?

A standard SMS message can contain up to **160 characters** when using GSM-7 encoding.

When a message exceeds the character limit, it is divided into multiple segments. Each segment counts toward your messaging usage and may be billed separately.

For example, a longer message may count as two or three segments even though it appears as a single message on your customer's phone.

You can use the [Message Segment Calculator](https://twiliodeved.github.io/message-segment-calculator/) to estimate how many segments your message will use.

## Why Do Message Segments Matter?

Longer messages use more segments, which can:

- Increase messaging costs.
- Make messages more difficult to read.
- Occasionally result in messages appearing separately on certain devices.
- Increase the risk of delivery issues when messages become excessively long.

<Warning>
  Some mobile carriers may block or reject SMS messages that exceed **10 segments**. To reduce the risk of delivery failures, consider shortening lengthy messages or sending the information as MMS instead.
</Warning>

## How Special Characters Affect Your Messages

Not all characters are treated the same by mobile carriers.

SMS messages primarily use two encoding standards:

- **GSM-7:** Supports standard letters, numbers, and many common symbols. A single SMS segment can contain up to 160 characters.
- **Unicode (UCS-2):** Supports emojis, additional symbols, and characters from many languages. A single SMS segment can contain up to 70 characters.

When a message contains characters that require Unicode encoding, the entire message may use the lower character limit.

### Common Characters That Can Affect Message Length

Examples include:

- **Emojis:** 😊 👍 ❤️ 🎉 ✅
- **Smart quotation marks:** “ ” ‘ ’
- **Long dashes:** — –
- **Ellipses:** …
- **Bullets:** •
- **Symbols:** ™ © ® ✓ ★ →
- **Certain accented characters:** č š ž ł
- **Non-Latin alphabets:** Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic, Hebrew, Cyrillic, and others
- **Invisible formatting characters:** Sometimes introduced when copying text from Word, Google Docs, or websites

Not every accented character requires Unicode. Common characters such as é, è, ñ, ü, ä, and ö are supported by GSM-7.

Some GSM-7 symbols, including `{ } [ ] \ ^ ~ | €`, count as two character positions.

There's nothing wrong with using special characters. Just be aware that they may increase the number of segments your message requires.

## Sending an MMS Instead of a Long SMS

If you need to share lengthy instructions, detailed information, or promotional content, consider converting your message into a **PNG image** and sending it as an MMS (Multimedia Messaging Service).

Unlike a text-only SMS, MMS allows you to include an image rather than sending all the information as multiple SMS segments.

### How to Send Your Message as an MMS

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create Your Message">
    Create your message in a document or design tool, such as Canva, Microsoft Word, or Google Docs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save as a PNG">
    Save or export your content as a **PNG image**. Make sure the text is large enough to read on a mobile phone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the File Size">
    Keep your image file **under 1 MB** to help ensure successful delivery.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open a Conversation">
    Open the customer's conversation in Captivated.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Attach Your Image">
    Select the attachment option in the message composer and upload your PNG.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a Short Introduction">
    Include a brief text message explaining what the image contains.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and Send">
    Confirm that your image is readable and contains the correct information before sending.
  </Step>
</Steps>

When you attach an image, Captivated sends the message as MMS rather than a text-only SMS.

### Best Practices for MMS

- **Keep it readable:** Make sure the text in your image is large enough to read on a mobile device.
- **Avoid overcrowding:** Don't place excessive amounts of information in a single image.
- **Include context:** Add a short text introduction explaining what you're sending.
- **Keep important information accessible:** Consider providing essential details in text or through a link as well.
- **Review before sending:** Preview your image to confirm it's clear and accurate.

For more information, see [Sending Attachments](/features/sending-attachments).

## Tips for Keeping SMS Messages Short

Here are a few ways to reduce unnecessary message segments:

- **Be concise:** Keep your messages clear, conversational, and focused.
- **Use emojis selectively:** Emojis can increase segment counts by changing the message's encoding.
- **Avoid unnecessary formatting:** Copying text from documents or websites can introduce special characters.
- **Use branded links:** If shortening a long URL, use a branded link associated with your business rather than public link shorteners such as bit.ly or TinyURL.
- **Review announcements:** Check message length before sending bulk announcements or marketing campaigns.
- **Consider MMS:** Use an image when lengthy instructions or promotional content would otherwise require numerous SMS segments.

## Message Segment Example

**Short Message**

> Hi John! Your lawn treatment is scheduled for tomorrow between 9:00-11:00 AM. Reply STOP to opt out.

**Longer Message**

> Hi John! We wanted to remind you about your scheduled lawn treatment tomorrow between 9:00 and 11:00 AM. Please remember to move any lawn furniture, children's toys, pet bowls, garden hoses, or other obstacles before our technician arrives. If you have any questions, simply reply to this message.

The second message contains more information and requires additional message segments.

While longer messages are sometimes necessary, keeping messages concise can help reduce costs and improve readability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Why did my message count as multiple text messages?">
    Your message may have exceeded the character limit for a single SMS or included characters that changed its encoding.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do emojis cost more?">
    Not directly. However, emojis typically require Unicode encoding, which reduces the number of characters available per segment. This may increase the total number of segments used.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Should I always stay under 160 characters?">
    Not necessarily. Your priority should be sending clear, helpful information.

    Messages can exceed 160 characters when necessary, but be mindful of segment counts and avoid excessively long SMS messages.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does sending an MMS eliminate message segment charges?">
    MMS is a different message type from SMS and may have different billing rates. Sending an image avoids dividing that image's content into multiple SMS text segments, but MMS messages may still incur messaging charges.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
